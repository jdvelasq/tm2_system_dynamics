The authors [UT 236] present a scoping review focused on the implementation of clinical services for adults with obesity across various health systems, utilizing a system dynamics framework with causal loop diagramming. Recognizing the persistent underrecognition of obesity’s medical needs and the limited maturity of corresponding clinical services, the study consolidates findings from 21 published articles to elucidate the complex interplay of factors influencing service utilization and delivery. By systematically identifying 19 key causal variables and mapping 38 unidirectional causal arrows, the authors construct a comprehensive causal loop diagram that centers on service provision and access within clinical obesity care. This diagrammatic analysis highlights critical feedback loops and uncovers potential intervention points essential for advancing service development, scale-up, and optimization. Notably, the study identifies actionable strategies such as raising awareness of obesity as a disease warranting medical attention, enhancing obesity-specific training for healthcare professionals, and integrating obesity-focused procedures into clinical protocols. These intervention points, derived from empirical evidence via causal loop analysis, offer valuable guidance for policymakers and health care administrators seeking to improve the effectiveness and reach of clinical obesity services. The work’s novel application of system dynamics provides nuanced insights into the structural and operational levers that can be engaged to bolster the implementation of adult obesity interventions across diverse healthcare settings.

The authors [UT 952] present a system dynamics-based simulation to evaluate the economic performance of construction and demolition waste (C&DW) reduction management, with a focus on the effective application of prefabrication techniques. The study is motivated by the growing environmental and economic challenges posed by increased C&DW resulting from intensified construction activities and evolving urban lifestyles. Using data collected from field surveys with project managers, quantity surveyors, and experienced site managers, the research compares traditional C&DW management strategies with those incorporating prefabrication. The simulation, developed in VENSIM PLE, models causal relationships among the key dependent and independent variables, integrating economic theories to assess both conventional and prefabricated construction methods. Key findings indicate that the adoption of prefabrication leads to a substantial reduction in both waste generation and overall management costs, with results showing that prefabrication can reduce C&DW management expenses by approximately 79% compared to conventional construction methods. This work underscores the significant economic and environmental benefits of prefabrication within the construction industry, offering a novel quantitative analysis through system dynamics modeling. The study contributes important evidence supporting prefabrication as an effective strategy for waste reduction and cost efficiency, thereby informing best practices for sustainable construction management.

The authors [UT 560] propose a system dynamics model integrating a PID (Proportional-Integral-Derivative) controller within a continuous review inventory management system (Q-system) to mitigate the adverse effects of demand shocks on customer service levels. By developing a feedback mechanism based on control loops, the study leverages the Vensim software platform to simulate the dynamic interaction between inventory control processes and the PID controller in a single-echelon logistics context. The model accounts for real-time adjustment of system parameters in response to fluctuating demand, thereby aiming to enhance resilience and maintain optimal service levels. The operational effectiveness of the proposed framework is demonstrated through comprehensive simulation experiments, providing quantitative insights into its ability to reduce the negative influence of demand shocks. This work offers a novel intersection of control theory and inventory management, particularly by applying electric control equipment techniques and controller algorithms to dynamic inventory systems under shock conditions. The findings contribute to the literature by illustrating the applicability of proportional control systems and term control systems in optimizing continuous review inventory systems in the presence of demand volatility.

The authors [UT 1145] conducted a systematic literature review to evaluate the current landscape of Software Process Simulation Modeling (SPSM) in the context of software risk management. Their analysis addresses the persistent challenges faced by software projects due to diverse software risks and identifies the limitations in real-world risk management practices, particularly the lack of effective tools accessible to project managers. The review reveals that, although the volume of SPSM-focused studies addressing software risk management remains modest, there is a noticeable upward trajectory in the number of contributions in recent years. The application of SPSM has been predominantly observed in risk analysis and risk management planning activities, with particular focus on risks associated with requirements, the development process, and the management process. The investigation highlights that Discrete-Event Simulation and System Dynamics are the prevailing simulation paradigms leveraged in this area, with hybrid simulation approaches gaining increased adoption. Among simulation tools, Extend, iThink, and Vensim emerge as the most frequently utilized platforms within the reviewed literature. Despite these advancements, the authors underscore a significant finding: most SPSM methodologies and models have yet to achieve substantial integration into practical, real-world software risk management environments. This systematic review thus provides critical insights into the evolving role and adoption patterns of SPSM, synthesizing key trends and gaps in the field, and furnishing a foundation for future empirical research and methodological refinement.

The authors [UT 1132] present a thorough literature review analyzing the evolution and advancements in dynamic urban ecosystem modeling (DUEM). Their work delineates the interdisciplinary nature of DUEM, encompassing scientific domains such as landscape ecology, urban demography, sociology, urban and environmental planning, environmental economics, disaster prevention science, as well as urban hygiene and public health. Tracing the origins of urban ecosystem modeling to the 19th century, the review highlights a significant methodological shift that occurred in the 1960s toward integrating ecological and environmental dimensions within urban system models. The authors classify the existing modeling approaches into four principal types: mathematical mechanistic models, eco-cybernetics based sensitivity models, system dynamics models, and multi-objective models, while also acknowledging the growing influence of supplementary methods such as ecological footprint analysis, scenario analysis, and entropy-based approaches. Despite methodological diversity, all models generally adhere to a six-step procedure, encompassing model definition, formulation, computer realization, calibration/validation, performance evaluation, and application. The review provides an overview of widely implemented models, distinguishing between those created via direct programming and those utilizing prominent simulation platforms such as VENSIM, STELLA, DYNAMO, and Matlab's Simulink toolbox. Addressing a crucial challenge, the paper discusses inherent uncertainties present throughout model development and application, advocating for enhanced strategies to manage uncertainty within DUEM research. Finally, the authors identify future research directions, emphasizing the need to expand modeling techniques to both macro and micro scales, and suggesting the integration of artificial intelligence and geographical information systems for hybrid model development.

The authors [UT 1149] present a comprehensive system dynamics (SD) model developed using VENSIM PLE to analyze the complex interactions between human and land systems within Xiangfan, Hubei Province, China, particularly in response to environmental catastrophes. Their work emphasizes the significance of achieving a harmonious relationship between society and the environment as a cornerstone of regional sustainable development. By simulating the dynamic responses of social and economic subsystems to disruptive environmental events, the study explores both the mechanisms underpinning sustainable development and the impact of major infrastructural interventions, specifically forecasting the long-term effects (over a 50-year horizon) of the south-to-north water transfer project on local environment, society, and economy. Notably, model outcomes reveal counterintuitive insights, such as the identification of economic growth, rather than environmental quality, as the most severely affected dimension resulting from the water transfer project. Additionally, the research demonstrates that implementing a proactive suite of regulatory policies prior to project commencement can significantly mitigate adverse trends. The study highlights both the utility and the current methodological limitations of the SD approach in dealing with intricately linked man-land systems, advocating for further integration of SD with advanced computational algorithms to enhance future modeling capabilities. This contribution underlines the relevance of dynamic response modeling for effective environmental risk assessment and the formulation of robust policy interventions in regional human-environment systems.
