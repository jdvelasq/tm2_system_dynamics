System dynamics is a methodological framework rooted in system theory, designed to model, simulate, and analyze the behavior of complex systems characterized by interdependent variables, feedback loops, time delays, and dynamic interactions among multiple subsystems [UT 136; UT 426; UT 297; UT 367; UT 373; UT 403; UT 140; UT 256; UT 74; UT 165; UT 238; UT 315; UT 447; UT 7; UT 11; UT 198; UT 203; UT 281; UT 295; UT 305; UT 345]. Employing both qualitative and quantitative modeling techniques—including causal loop diagrams, stock-and-flow structures, mathematical simulations, and computer-based tools—system dynamics integrates empirical data, structural analysis, and parameter estimation to elucidate causal relationships, feedback mechanisms, and emergent system behavior [UT 259; UT 73; UT 316; UT 280; UT 62; UT 74; UT 84; UT 101; UT 186; UT 200; UT 206; UT 305]. Specialized software platforms such as Vensim, Powersim, Stella Architect, and MATLAB Simulink support the construction and simulation of models spanning diverse domains including health, energy, infrastructure, resource management, environmental systems, supply chains, policy analysis, transportation, and innovation ecosystems [UT 83; UT 30; UT 99; UT 173; UT 297; UT 367; UT 316; UT 84; UT 396; UT 279; UT 220; UT 226; UT 334]. This approach facilitates the evaluation of policy scenarios, optimization of resource allocation, risk assessment, strategic intervention, and scenario analysis by revealing dynamic interactions, critical leverage points, and subsystem coupling within integrated sociotechnical and organizational environments [UT 259; UT 173; UT 462; UT 13; UT 319; UT 255; UT 50; UT 182; UT 383; UT 393].
