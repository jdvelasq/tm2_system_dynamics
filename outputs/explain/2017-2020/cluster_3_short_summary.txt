This cluster focuses on the intersection of system dynamics modeling, economic incentives, and policy mechanisms to guide and assess the transition of energy systems—particularly renewable energy—through the analytical lens of subsidies, tradable green certificates, and regulatory instruments such as feed-in tariffs (FIT) and renewable portfolio standards (RPS) [UT 580][UT 687][UT 684][UT 591][UT 947][UT 999][UT 686][UT 884][UT 982][UT 641][UT 996]. Methodological advances are marked by increasingly sophisticated simulation-based scenario analysis that integrates economic, technical, and socio-environmental subsystems, enabling detailed exploration of time-dependent relationships among policy interventions, stakeholder behaviors, technology costs, and environmental outcomes. Empirical breadth has expanded—often illustrated by the well-studied Chinese context—with scenario analyses revealing how combinations of market trading platforms, subsidy schemes, and willingness-to-pay thresholds influence renewable deployment trajectories, including strategic targets like 300 GW PV capacity by 2030 [UT 996]. Despite this progress, significant challenges remain: key economic, social, and technological feedbacks are hard to accurately represent when variables such as product prices or exploitation costs are poorly defined or insufficiently monitored, hindering robust model calibration and limiting generalizability, especially amid highly diverse regulatory and market environments [UT 580][UT 947][UT 686][UT 884][UT 982][UT 641]. Research opportunities lie in integrating real-time data, cross-sectoral linkages, and participatory approaches for richer, more adaptive modeling—expanding both the granularity and scope of models to capture the nuanced effects of varied subsidy and tradable mechanisms across regions and sectors [UT 591][UT 947][UT 982][UT 996]. Further, embedding considerations of equity, social welfare, and just transitions within these frameworks would position system dynamics as a holistic decision-support tool for sustainable energy policy [UT 686][UT 982]. Practically, enhancing the transparency, usability, and stakeholder inclusiveness of system dynamics models—through intuitive interfaces, robust validation, and integration with sustainable development indicators—will maximize their actionable value for policymaking, ensuring subsidy and renewable policy interventions are both effective and adaptable within complex, evolving energy landscapes [UT 580][UT 684][UT 691][UT 996][UT 999][UT 947].
