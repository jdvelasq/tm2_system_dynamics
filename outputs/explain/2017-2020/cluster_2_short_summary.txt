This cluster is distinguished by the use of system dynamics to unravel the multifaceted and dynamically complex interactions among water resources, agriculture, and climate systems across diverse regional contexts, with water—including its supply, demand, quality, and groundwater sustainability—serving as the unifying concept that links hydrological, ecological, and socio-economic subsystems [UT 514][UT 508][UT 797][UT 618][UT 580][UT 632][UT 690][UT 607][UT 802][UT 868][UT 893][UT 810][UT 543][UT 787][UT 681][UT 637][UT 582]. Recent trends underscore a shift toward greater regional specificity, methodological sophistication, and the integration of scenario-based analysis, as dynamic models increasingly capture the intricacies of water management in urban, agricultural, and groundwater-dependent systems while broadening to include economic, land-use, and social impacts [UT 893][UT 591][UT 810][UT 543][UT 868][UT 787][UT 648][UT 681][UT 637][UT 582]. Persistent challenges emerge primarily from the conceptual and empirical difficulties of representing non-linear feedbacks between climate, agriculture, groundwater, and regional economies, as well as the scarcity and inconsistency of data, uncertainty in climate projections, stakeholder heterogeneity, and implementation hurdles that complicate the translation of model insights into actionable water and agricultural policies [UT 810][UT 543][UT 868][UT 787][UT 514][UT 508][UT 618][UT 690][UT 632][UT 607][UT 802][UT 893][UT 648][UT 582]. Significant research opportunities lie in developing integrative frameworks that accommodate spatial-biophysical heterogeneity, better couple system dynamics with agent-based and optimization models, enhance model realism with real-time and stakeholder-generated data, and incorporate explicit consideration of technology diffusion, market changes, and adaptive policy feedbacks, particularly in water-scarce, groundwater-dependent, or rapidly urbanizing regions [UT 797][UT 514][UT 868][UT 683][UT 787][UT 681][UT 690][UT 810][UT 580][UT 632][UT 893][UT 543][UT 648][UT 582][UT 559]. For enhancing practical value, a participatory, user-centered approach is essential, involving co-development of models with stakeholders to ensure relevance, transparency, and buy-in, coupled with robust calibration using high-frequency data, modular and scalable platforms, educational initiatives, and structured feedback mechanisms that promote adaptive governance and facilitate the real-world translation of dynamic modeling into effective regional water and agriculture management strategies [UT 648][UT 580][UT 514][UT 632][UT 608][UT 802][UT 810][UT 893][UT 543][UT 868][UT 683][UT 681][UT 787][UT 637][UT 559].
