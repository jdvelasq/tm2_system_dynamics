This thematic cluster is defined by its focus on the intersection between system dynamics modeling and accident phenomena, particularly in high-risk domains where safety is a primary concern [UT 883, UT 715, UT 746, UT 504, UT 587]. System dynamics provides a systemic approach to the origination, mitigation, and consequences of accidents within organizational and technical systems, employing simulation to elucidate the dynamic, feedback-driven processes that shape accident frequency and severity. Recent trends reveal increasing methodological sophistication and thematic breadth, as system dynamics models now address accident risks across diverse settings—including construction, chemical, petrochemical, and nuclear sectors—and incorporate nuanced variables such as safety culture, governmental credibility, public sentiment, production pressure, and group behaviors [UT 883, UT 715, UT 746, UT 504, UT 587]. This evolution is marked by the decomposition of safety investments, dynamic human error probability assessments, and scenario-based sensitivity analyses, reflecting a transition toward capturing socio-behavioral complexities alongside technical failures. Nevertheless, the field faces conceptual and methodological challenges stemming from the intricacy of feedback loops, system non-linearities, and the entanglement of human error, organizational commitment, and risk perception, which limit the identification of clear causal relationships and reliable policy levers [UT 715, UT 746, UT 504, UT 587]. Limitations in quantifying and calibrating core factors, coupled with generalizability concerns due to reliance on scenario-specific assumptions, further constrain the robustness and external validity of accident models—especially in rare-event contexts such as nuclear emergencies [UT 587]. Future research should advance the integration of human and organizational dynamics, formalize the modeling of safety culture and group behavior, and expand the application of system dynamics to broader accident typologies and public health emergencies, while also improving quantification and empirical validation [UT 504, UT 715, UT 746, UT 883]. Cross-contextual analyses and real-time simulation techniques offer promising directions to enhance predictive power and policy relevance. Practically, the value of this cluster can be amplified by intensifying empirical validation efforts with real-world data, fostering expert collaboration for contextual accuracy, developing user-oriented decision-support tools, and ensuring transparent documentation of model assumptions and limitations. These measures will strengthen the implementation of system dynamics insights for accident prevention and mitigation in complex socio-technical environments [UT 883, UT 504, UT 746, UT 715, UT 587].
