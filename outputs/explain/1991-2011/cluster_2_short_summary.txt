The application of system dynamics (SD) methodologies to water, river, and reservoir systems forms a dynamic, multi-disciplinary thematic family dedicated to the quantitative analysis, simulation, and management of interconnected water resources across a spectrum of spatial and temporal scales. This cluster is defined by its robust conceptual framework that integrates water balance, allocation, and management with environmental and socio-economic feedbacks, connecting hydrological processes such as infiltration, groundwater interactions, evapotranspiration, and reservoir operations with human interventions including irrigation practices, allocation rules, and infrastructure management [UT 796][UT 515][UT 677][UT 702][UT 649][UT 1049][UT 1094][UT 1111][UT 1074][UT 1021][UT 1135][UT 1067][UT 1005][UT 1069][UT 1098][UT 1115][UT 1081][UT 1006][UT 1117][UT 1019][UT 1016]. Recent trends showcase a maturation in SD modeling, marked by advances that move beyond static representations toward feedback-rich, stakeholder-oriented frameworks capable of incorporating ecological, economic, and technological dimensions [UT 1085][UT 1060]. Notably, SD’s integration with uncertainty quantification, scenario analysis, and stakeholder engagement has improved policy relevance and the applicability of simulation studies to diverse settings—from local irrigation districts to national river basin management—while rigor in model validation and empirical application continues to rise. However, the cluster faces enduring challenges: chief among these are the conceptual difficulty of accurately modeling complex, non-linear feedbacks across hydrological and socio-economic domains, methodological issues related to parameterization, validation, and integration of heterogeneous data, and practical issues such as fragmented stakeholder responsibilities and limited model accessibility [UT 1135][UT 1006][UT 1060][UT 1098]. Addressing these obstacles invites research opportunities focused on enhancing spatial-temporal modeling by coupling SD with distributed hydrologic frameworks, incorporating ecological security indicators, integrating advanced AI-based optimization, and leveraging multi-scale, participatory model validation to inform sustainable management under climatic and demographic pressures [UT 1085][UT 1019][UT 1016][UT 1117][UT 1098]. To amplify practical value, future efforts should prioritize the development of accessible decision-support tools and virtual laboratories, foster cross-sectoral collaboration, ensure robust stakeholder engagement, and embed adaptive, real-time management capabilities that integrate ecological, economic, and climate resilience concerns, thereby supporting more actionable, accountable, and sustainable stewardship of water, river, and reservoir resources [UT 1135][UT 1069][UT 1098][UT 1115].
