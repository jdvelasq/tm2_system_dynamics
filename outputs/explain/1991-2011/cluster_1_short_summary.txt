This cluster centers on the application of system dynamics to elucidate and manage the intricacies of contemporary supply chains, placing particular emphasis on the dynamic interplay among supply processes, customer behavior, and electronic information flows. At its core, the cluster advances understanding of the bullwhip effect by analyzing feedback loops and time delays across multi-echelon, short life cycle, and electronics-focused supply systems, where factors such as order batching, demand amplification, supplier strategies, inventory management, and customer satisfaction intersect [UT 965][UT 966][UT 902][UT 1076][UT 1072][UT 1040][UT 1121][UT 909][UT 1043][UT 1065][UT 1093][UT 1078][UT 804]. Recent trends reveal a methodological shift from isolated, supply-centric analyses toward integrated, empirically grounded simulations that factor in customer-centric variables and the disruptive potential of electronic commerce, with models calibrated and refined through real market data and industry collaboration [UT 1040][UT 1043][UT 1121][UT 909][UT 1065][UT 1093][UT 804][UT 1088]. However, persistent challenges remain in accurately capturing and operationalizing the non-linear and often intangible dynamics of supply-customer interactions—such as customer satisfaction, complaint management, and lead-time perceptions—while addressing limitations in data accessibility, model scalability, and practitioner engagement with simulation-driven insights [UT 1088][UT 1084][UT 1122][UT 1043][UT 1011][UT 1121][UT 1093][UT 909][UT 1040][UT 1065][UT 1078]. The future of research in this family lies in integrating high-resolution, real-world data to enhance model robustness; expanding the incorporation of qualitative and electronic feedback mechanisms, including IoT and digital communication; and systematically validating models across sectors—ranging from industrial and food sectors to telecommunications and urban retail—to ensure broad applicability and resilience in the face of disruptive events [UT 804][UT 1088][UT 1043][UT 965][UT 949][UT 924][UT 902][UT 1072][UT 1121][UT 1093][UT 909][UT 1011][UT 1122]. Practically, the value of system dynamics can be maximized by aligning simulation models tightly with industry realities, leveraging participatory modeling and interactive scenario exploration, embedding simulation-driven decision support within electronic platforms, and continuously updating models with feedback from operational data and stakeholders. By fostering industry-academic collaboration and embedding transparent, feedback-enabled models in practice, system dynamics research is poised to deliver tangible improvements in supply chain resilience, cost management, and sustained customer satisfaction.
