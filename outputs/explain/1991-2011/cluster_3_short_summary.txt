This cluster centers on the conceptual and methodological integration of system dynamics to model and analyze the complex interactions among soil, water, and the associated fluxes of evapotranspiration across natural and managed ecosystems. Rooted in physically based and empirical formulations, models within this thematic family dynamically simulate core hydrological processes—including infiltration, percolation, soil evaporation, root water uptake, capillary rise, runoff, and drainage—in a variety of spatio-temporal contexts ranging from multilayered soils and agricultural fields to reconstructed watersheds and basin-wide systems [UT 696][UT 1049][UT 1051][UT 1094][UT 1074][UT 1117][UT 880][UT 1006]. Recent trends underscore a notable expansion in both the scope and sophistication of these models: initial efforts at the field scale, such as simulating crop-specific root zone water balances, have evolved toward integrative, basin-level analyses and ecosystem-scale assessments, leveraging scenario-driven approaches for sustainable water management and the examination of complex processes like lake salinization and land reclamation. This evolution has emphasized holistic frameworks over compartmentalized methods, integrating key variables—such as actual evapotranspiration, capillary fluxes, and feedbacks across system components—while shifting the focus from prediction alone to decision-support, exemplified by scenario analysis for policy and management evaluation [UT 696][UT 1049][UT 1094][UT 1074][UT 1006][UT 880]. Nevertheless, significant conceptual and methodological challenges remain, particularly regarding the accurate representation of non-linear, bidirectional feedbacks, spatial heterogeneity, and multi-scale interactions among soil, water, and evapotranspiration [UT 1094][UT 880][UT 1049][UT 1074][UT 1117][UT 1006]. These issues are compounded by persistent data limitations—especially for model calibration and validation—which hinder both predictive reliability and the broader transferability of insights. Looking forward, research opportunities include advancing the spatial explicitness of models, broadening the physical and empirical modules to capture additional cycles (such as nutrients and salts), enhancing robustness under climatic extremes through uncertainty quantification, and fostering stakeholder-driven, participatory modeling processes aligned with technological innovations in remote sensing and data assimilation [UT 1006][UT 1094][UT 880][UT 1074][UT 1049][UT 1117][UT 1051]. To maximize practical value, it is recommended that future system dynamics applications systematically expand validation across diverse contexts, develop user-friendly interfaces, integrate scenario-based decision tools, and facilitate knowledge exchange among research, policy, and practice. Collectively, these directions will enable more reliable, scalable, and actionable system dynamics models for the sustainable management of soil, water, and evapotranspiration systems.
