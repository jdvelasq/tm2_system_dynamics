This system dynamics cluster is defined by its focus on the integrated modeling and management of sustainability issues regarding water, agricultural, economic, waste, groundwater, and catchment domains, emphasizing the complex feedbacks and nonlinear interactions across these interlinked subsystems [UT 1094][UT 1111][UT 1135][UT 1129][UT 1095][UT 1055][UT 1020][UT 1018]. Recent research trends highlight a shift from isolated analyses toward holistic, scenario-driven models that capture the full spectrum of biophysical, economic, and policy factors, often leveraging empirical, region-specific data to address real-world sustainability challenges [UT 1095][UT 1135][UT 1129][UT 1055][UT 1094][UT 1111]. These advances are underscored by the adoption of iterative, adaptive approaches for multi-objective policy assessment, encompassing crop selection, water use, land allocation, and waste stream regulation under dynamic pressures from climate change, population growth, and regulatory complexity [UT 1135][UT 1069][UT 1006][UT 1060][UT 1115][UT 1081][UT 1047]. Nevertheless, the field is impeded by significant conceptual and implementation challenges, including difficulties in system boundary definition, capturing nonlinear feedbacks, empirical data scarcity, inadequate representation of distributed hydrological and heterogeneous land-use processes, and fragmented governance that hampers integrated management efforts [UT 1134][UT 1052][UT 1016][UT 1149][UT 1059][UT 1060][UT 1111]. Further, effective translation of model insights into actionable policy is hindered by limited stakeholder engagement, communication barriers, and persistent uncertainties in model predictions [UT 1059][UT 1060][UT 1149]. There are promising opportunities for future research in advancing spatially explicit, cross-scale, and participatory models, integrating behavioral and stakeholder-driven dynamics, and employing hybrid methodologies to address multi-objective trade-offs and uncertainty—particularly through deeper empirical calibration and validation [UT 1134][UT 1052][UT 1129][UT 1135][UT 1019][UT 1070][UT 1117][UT 1081][UT 1149]. To maximize practical impact, it is recommended that model development prioritize participatory, user-friendly decision-support features, empirical data integration, and clear visualization tools to bridge the gap between technical findings and adaptive management—while dedicated training and collaborative initiatives can ensure ongoing, context-sensitive application of system dynamics for sustainable water, catchment, agricultural, and waste management [UT 1094][UT 1135][UT 1047][UT 1081][UT 1115][UT 1019].
