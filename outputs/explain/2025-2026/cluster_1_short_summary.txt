Defined by the integrated application of system dynamics modeling, this thematic cluster holistically addresses the sustainability challenges present across agricultural, food, water, economic, climate, and population systems, with a pronounced focus on coordinated management and resilience in arid as well as diverse geographic areas [UT 30][UT 99][UT 297][UT 321][UT 316][UT 160][UT 429][UT 58][UT 170][UT 238][UT 255][UT 101][UT 383][UT 17][UT 175][UT 186][UT 226][UT 295][UT 305][UT 312][UT 314][UT 390][UT 281][UT 398][UT 402][UT 404][UT 438][UT 443]. Recent trends reflect a proliferation of system dynamics as a methodological backbone for investigating multi-sectoral sustainability, characterized by scenario-based simulation and coordinated modeling of resource flows in food, water, energy, and agricultural systems—often operationalized through empirical analyses of circular economy practices, food redistribution networks, and climate adaptation in water-scarce or rapidly urbanizing regions [UT 429][UT 321][UT 297][UT 305][UT 226][UT 58][UT 84][UT 404][UT 438]. Despite expanding thematic breadth, persistent challenges endure, including the capture of nonlinear feedbacks, data uncertainties, limited empirical datasets, balancing model granularity with computational feasibility, and practical impediments surrounding stakeholder alignment, policy inertia, and implementation in both sustainable supply chains and agricultural transitions [UT 395][UT 144][UT 30][UT 58][UT 84][UT 170][UT 314][UT 398][UT 402][UT 393][UT 17][UT 101][UT 443][UT 11][UT 307]. Research opportunities emphasize enhancing models with high-frequency, longitudinal, and real-time data, advancing integration of circular economy feedbacks, hybridizing methodologies, and investigating underexplored social-institutional and cross-sectoral dynamics, particularly in the context of arid agriculture, population pressures, and climate-driven resource variability [UT 429][UT 305][UT 321][UT 186][UT 281][UT 314][UT 238][UT 170][UT 305][UT 404][UT 398][UT 438][UT 443]. Advancing this cluster’s practical value demands strengthened stakeholder participation in model development and validation, co-design of actionable decision-support tools, and capacity building to democratize system dynamics—and should also prioritize transparent communication, user-centric visualizations, and open sharing of assumptions and results—to ensure robust, equitable, and adaptive resource and supply management under evolving climate and demographic conditions [UT 30][UT 321][UT 429][UT 17][UT 393][UT 305][UT 58][UT 84][UT 398][UT 160][UT 297][UT 390][UT 312][UT 314][UT 186][UT 443][UT 438].
