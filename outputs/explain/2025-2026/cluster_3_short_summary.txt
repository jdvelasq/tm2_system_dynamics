This thematic cluster centers on the application of system dynamics as a methodological and conceptual foundation for unraveling and optimizing complex health processes, integrating diverse subjects such as public health, epidemiology, disease prevalence, cost analysis, prevention strategies, vaccination, and environmental determinants including landfill management and emergent threats like the coronavirus [UT 403][UT 140][UT 65][UT 462][UT 301][UT 429][UT 370][UT 13][UT 270][UT 125][UT 200][UT 236][UT 291][UT 344][UT 345][UT 351][UT 438]. Recent trends illustrate an increasingly holistic and interdisciplinary research paradigm, expanding system dynamics’ reach beyond infectious disease modeling to encompass hospital resource planning, chronic and communicable disease management, and the nexus of food, waste, and public health policies [UT 403][UT 429][UT 301][UT 370][UT 462]. With richer integrations of socioeconomic, age-stratified, and patient-centric parameters, models now concertedly assess prevention, prevalence, and cost in diverse populations, while advances in empirical calibration and scenario simulation have facilitated more actionable responses to urgent phenomena, such as COVID-19 [UT 140][UT 65][UT 351][UT 344][UT 462]. However, substantial challenges persist, particularly regarding methodological rigor in model calibration and validation amid data limitations, the integration of behavioral, social, and environmental complexity, and the translation of simulated outcomes into actionable, ethically robust policy—especially when facing institutional inertia, stakeholder resistance, or ethical dilemmas inherent in resource allocation and vaccine distribution [UT 345][UT 236][UT 270][UT 301][UT 370][UT 438][UT 403][UT 200][UT 291]. Major research opportunities emphasize bridging policy, empirical, and stakeholder gaps: integrating behavioral and environmental determinants, leveraging real-time and routinely collected data for dynamic decision support, modeling cross-sectoral feedbacks—such as between landfill-reduction initiatives and community health—and advancing the use of system dynamics for equitable, cost-effective innovation in areas like genomics and preventive care [UT 65][UT 140][UT 462][UT 13][UT 301][UT 344][UT 351][UT 429][UT 370][UT 438][UT 291][UT 403]. To maximize practical value, sustained participatory model co-design, enhanced stakeholder engagement, and multi-country validation are recommended—ensuring system dynamics models are contextually relevant, empirically sound, and policy-responsive, while rigorous data integration and standardized workflows promote interpretability, credibility, and uptake to inform sustainable health, prevention, cost management, and environmental intervention strategies [UT 429][UT 370][UT 438][UT 200][UT 345][UT 236][UT 462][UT 65][UT 301][UT 140][UT 291][UT 344][UT 351][UT 125][UT 13].
