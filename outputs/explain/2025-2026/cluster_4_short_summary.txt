Within this thematic cluster, system dynamics serves as the foundational framework for the dynamic analysis and management of risk and safety, with a distinctive emphasis on risk coupling—the complex, interdependent interactions among multiple socio-technical subsystems that characterize domains such as engineering, construction, mining, transportation, and finance [UT 367][UT 50][UT 182][UT 120][UT 183][UT 334][UT 357][UT 458]. The field has evolved from static, single-factor assessments toward sophisticated, dynamic multi-factor modeling that brings to the forefront the interplay of feedback loops, emergent behaviors, and coupled risk factors—including human, technical, organizational, and environmental dimensions—affecting safety performance. Recent trends highlight the integration of system dynamics with advanced quantitative tools to enhance modeling precision and spatial-temporal resolution, coupled with empirical validation through case studies and field data [UT 182][UT 183][UT 334][UT 458]. However, significant challenges persist: accurately capturing and quantifying the heterogeneity and emergent properties inherent in risk coupling, translating qualitative causal relationships into robust quantitative systems, and addressing data limitations, modeling sensitivities, and scalability issues in real-world applications [UT 120][UT 367][UT 182][UT 357][UT 50][UT 334][UT 183]. In response, future research should advance multi-factor, adaptive modeling frameworks by leveraging digital simulation, artificial intelligence, big data analytics, and cross-disciplinary integration (e.g., SD-GIS) to improve context-specific risk management, especially in dynamic operational environments [UT 357][UT 334][UT 367][UT 458]. Additionally, participatory approaches involving diverse stakeholders offer potential for enhancing the practical value, transferability, and iterative refinement of system dynamics models [UT 50][UT 367][UT 357]. To maximize organizational benefit, it is practically recommended to embed model-driven decision support systems directly into risk management protocols, promote integrated emergency plans that reflect risk coupling, and establish continuous empirical validation and feedback loops, fostering a culture of safety learning and responsive, proactive risk mitigation across sectors [UT 358][UT 183][UT 357][UT 50][UT 367][UT 334][UT 458][UT 120].
