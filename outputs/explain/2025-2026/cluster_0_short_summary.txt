This thematic cluster is defined by its comprehensive use of system dynamics to address the intricate challenges of carbon reduction, technological innovation, prefabricated construction, energy management, and green transformation within diverse sectors such as construction, urban transportation, manufacturing, and energy systems. At its core, the research builds upon dynamic methodological frameworks to simulate and forecast multifaceted interactions between carbon emissions, energy consumption, regulatory market instruments, technological advances, and policy interventions—including emissions trading, green subsidies, and zero-carbon market mechanisms [UT 426, UT 136, UT 395, UT 73, UT 125, UT 76, UT 260, UT 160, UT 316, UT 429; UT 222, UT 255, UT 418, UT 50, UT 170, UT 407, UT 27, UT 31, UT 165, UT 315; UT 279, UT 288, UT 374, UT 381, UT 458]. Recent trends indicate an escalating sophistication and interdisciplinary depth, with increasingly granular, internationalized, and integrated approaches that account for multi-scalar feedbacks, scenario planning, and the structural coupling of policy, technological, and market subsystems [UT 426, UT 136, UT 160, UT 73, UT 260, UT 125, UT 76, UT 136, UT 316, UT 395, UT 429, UT 279, UT 381, UT 458, UT 334]. However, persistent challenges remain, notably the difficulty of operationalizing complex, multi-level systems, addressing data sufficiency, validating heterogeneous stakeholder behaviors, and ensuring robust empirical calibration and policy coordination under varied regulatory landscapes [UT 426, UT 316, UT 160, UT 418, UT 170, UT 374, UT 381, UT 136, UT 407, UT 395, UT 255, UT 31, UT 407, UT 381, UT 334, UT 351, UT 374, UT 170, UT 27, UT 429, UT 160, UT 125, UT 260, UT 76, UT 316, UT 315, UT 50, UT 288, UT 458]. Building on these insights, current research opportunities call for the expansion of hybrid, multi-method system dynamics models capable of capturing the behavioral, infrastructural, and sectoral heterogeneity of zero-carbon transitions, as well as the design and empirical evaluation of policy bundles and scalable interventions across differing institutional contexts [UT 260, UT 426, UT 27, UT 288, UT 315, UT 255, UT 288, UT 374, UT 334, UT 316, UT 160, UT 76, UT 136, UT 31, UT 407, UT 381, UT 355, UT 73, UT 125, UT 50, UT 165, UT 351, UT 429, UT 395, UT 418, UT 170, UT 334]. To enhance the cluster’s practical value, it is crucial to embed participatory model co-design, robust stakeholder engagement, user-centric tool dissemination, adaptive scenario recalibration, and institutionalized capacity building—across policy, industry, and community settings—thus ensuring that simulation findings lead to actionable, robust, and context-responsive strategies for global decarbonization and green market transformation [UT 407, UT 31, UT 288, UT 395, UT 429, UT 418, UT 255, UT 279, UT 381, UT 125, UT 160, UT 27, UT 260, UT 316, UT 355, UT 458, UT 76, UT 50, UT 355, UT 334, UT 165, UT 170, UT 315].
