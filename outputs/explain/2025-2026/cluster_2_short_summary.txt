This cluster is grounded in the application of system dynamics to unravel the intricate, interdependent mechanisms governing carbon, energy, and power systems—encompassing renewable and fossil-based resources such as solar, photovoltaic, battery, and coal technologies—as well as their roles in greenhouse gas mitigation and sustainable energy transitions [UT 259][UT 297][UT 300][UT 160][UT 255][UT 170][UT 165][UT 315]. The current research trend demonstrates a marked progression toward integrated, multi-scalar models that scrutinize both supply-side (photovoltaic, solar, battery, coal) and demand-side (household adoption, electric vehicles) transformations, using scenario analysis to explore policy, technological, and behavioral impacts across urban, industrial, and microgrid contexts [UT 279][UT 381][UT 399][UT 412][UT 438][UT 130][UT 73][UT 393]. These advances are paralleled by deeper cross-sectoral analyses (e.g., water-energy-food-carbon nexus) and empirical validation, increasingly tailored to local socio-economic and climatic realities [UT 326][UT 120][UT 238][UT 173][UT 297]. However, the field faces substantive challenges: conceptual complexity in modeling feedbacks among sectoral variables, methodological obstacles such as embedding key drivers in scenario planning, data limitations affecting calibration and validation, and implementation barriers including policy misalignments, grid modernization deficits, and behavioral inertia stymieing solar, battery, and coal transitions [UT 399][UT 381][UT 279][UT 297][UT 260][UT 393][UT 412][UT 438][UT 160][UT 130]. These issues highlight the necessity for more adaptive, data-rich, and context-aware models capable of accommodating real-world variability. Future research should thus prioritize the development of multi-layered, empirically robust system dynamics frameworks that simultaneously capture sectoral coupling (including green hydrogen, circular economy, and distributed storage), refine policy instrument design, and integrate social and regulatory dimensions—particularly in emerging markets and underrepresented regions [UT 259][UT 297][UT 170][UT 238][UT 260][UT 173][UT 399][UT 255][UT 315][UT 326][UT 412][UT 438]. To maximize practical value, it is recommended that stakeholders employ participatory modeling techniques and real-time data integration to foster model relevance and adaptability, explicitly linking system dynamics outputs to actionable decarbonization, market, and policy objectives via user-friendly visualization platforms and transparent multi-criteria assessments, thereby fortifying the utility of these tools in guiding carbon management and energy innovation at the coal-renewable interface [UT 130][UT 173][UT 300][UT 73][UT 373][UT 381][UT 438][UT 160][UT 76][UT 297][UT 255][UT 393][UT 165][UT 175][UT 279].
