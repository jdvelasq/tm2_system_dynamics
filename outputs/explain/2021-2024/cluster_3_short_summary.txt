This thematic cluster centers on the strategic application of system dynamics to model, simulate, and optimize carbon emissions and energy consumption, with a particular emphasis on the construction, industrial, and supply chain sectors [UT 214, UT 368, UT 495, UT 3, UT 14, UT 209, UT 188, UT 435, UT 243, UT 194; UT 371, UT 44, UT 45, UT 215, UT 264]. The field is marked by its evolution from sector-specific analyses toward integrated, cross-sectoral approaches that holistically link carbon, supply, energy, and contractor behaviors within broader industrial and prefabricated construction systems [UT 435, UT 495, UT 214, UT 368, UT 14]. Recent trends underscore the adoption of increasingly sophisticated, multi-factorial models that account for feedback loops among economic, technological, and policy variables, aspiring to align operational practices with international low-carbon competitiveness and sustainability policy objectives [UT 243, UT 209, UT 44, UT 371, UT 45, UT 43, UT 228, UT 95, UT 155, UT 346, UT 427, UT 199, UT 121]. Despite these advances, the cluster grapples with conceptual and methodological challenges, such as delimiting system boundaries amidst overlapping emission sources, integrating multi-dimensional and often uncertain datasets, and translating simulation outputs into actionable policy—compounded particularly in prefabricated or international supply contexts [UT 188, UT 214, UT 435, UT 44, UT 264, UT 52, UT 95, UT 228; UT 371, UT 44, UT 258, UT 310, UT 354, UT 332, UT 346, UT 234, UT 63]. To overcome these barriers, future research must prioritize the development of multi-agent, multi-criteria, and cross-regional system dynamics models that incorporate real-time analytics, robust life cycle assessment, and dynamic risk coupling—facilitated by BIM, IoT, and other technological advancements [UT 148, UT 121, UT 248, UT 289, UT 234, UT 349, UT 371, UT 296, UT 377]. Empirical integration, scenario-based planning, and adaptive policy intervention will be essential in refining modeling accuracy and policy relevance, particularly to address market resistance, stakeholder inertia, and information asymmetry [UT 3, UT 371, UT 332, UT 271, UT 441, UT 263, UT 273, UT 310, UT 188, UT 248, UT 495]. Practically, the literature recommends closer stakeholder collaboration through participatory modeling, transparent and user-friendly tool development, and continuous calibration of interventions, ensuring that system dynamics–based recommendations are operationalized in both policy review and daily decision-making [UT 495, UT 214, UT 368, UT 194, UT 441, UT 332, UT 234, UT 349, UT 243, UT 44, UT 264]. Collectively, this cluster signifies an increasingly integrated paradigm that, by embedding carbon, emission, supply, construction, contractor, and industrial variables, holds substantial promise for scaling effective carbon mitigation strategies and achieving sustainable development goals in the face of evolving technological and regulatory landscapes.
