This thematic cluster centers on the application of system dynamics modeling to investigate and optimize the intricate, feedback-driven relationships among water resources, agriculture, climate, and sustainability within complex socio-economic and environmental contexts [UT 275][UT 253][UT 145][UT 116][UT 139, UT 324, UT 492, UT 379, UT 45, UT 287, UT 274, UT 306, UT 486, UT 169]. It is demarcated by holistic models that synthesize empirical data and scenario analyses to represent intersectoral water allocation, agricultural productivity, economic outcomes, crop selection, water demand and supply, reservoir and groundwater management, food security, and the multidimensional trade-offs present at micro and macro scales [UT 25][UT 69][UT 113][UT 132][UT 195][UT 43][UT 156][UT 124][UT 190][UT 207, UT 415, UT 91, UT 431, UT 187][UT 231, UT 135, UT 434, UT 496, UT 331]. Recent trends highlight a surge toward more methodologically sophisticated, scenario-based models capable of simulating the impacts of climate variability, drought, salinity, population growth, and evolving agricultural practices, thereby extending the system boundaries to include energy, economic, and regulatory dimensions, and accounting for water quantity and quality, sustainability indices, and ecosystem services [UT 275][UT 145][UT 427][UT 469][UT 156][UT 139][UT 274][UT 306]. However, significant challenges persist, including the accurate representation of nonlinear feedbacks across water, groundwater, cropping, and economic systems, limited empirical data for model calibration and validation, scaling issues from local to regional levels, and integrating social, policy, and behavioral factors amid site-specific constraints and climate uncertainties [UT 69][UT 440][UT 469][UT 18][UT 91][UT 187][UT 379][UT 169][UT 306][UT 81][UT 254][UT 113][UT 244][UT 232][UT 233][UT 208][UT 378][UT 405]. Despite these hurdles, research opportunities abound in refining dynamic frameworks to tightly couple biophysical, social, and economic processes; expanding scenario modeling under climate extremes; developing participatory, data-rich, and adaptive approaches; and bridging micro-macro decision-making to achieve more transferable, robust, and equitable solutions for water, agricultural, and food system sustainability [UT 287, UT 486, UT 335][UT 287, UT 486, UT 335][UT 169][UT 91][UT 197][UT 242][UT 195][UT 43][UT 275][UT 15][UT 252][UT 231][UT 40][UT 254][UT 208][UT 378][UT 405]. To enhance the practical impact of this cluster, it is recommended that research prioritize systematic stakeholder engagement, investment in real-time data infrastructures and modular model architectures, and explicit integration of policy levers and cross-sector scenario analyses, while fostering interdisciplinary collaboration to institutionalize adaptive management and generate actionable, context-specific strategies for sustainable water, agriculture, and climate resilience [UT 91, UT 187, UT 139, UT 379, UT 287, UT 38, UT 440, UT 156, UT 262, UT 69, UT 132, UT 113, UT 15][UT 253, UT 275, UT 492, UT 486, UT 496, UT 91, UT 81, UT 15, UT 108, UT 469, UT 415, UT 431][UT 274, UT 324, UT 169, UT 379, UT 45, UT 492, UT 231, UT 135, UT 244, UT 207, UT 242][UT 25, UT 195, UT 124, UT 43, UT 262, UT 113, UT 25, UT 195, UT 232, UT 460, UT 388, UT 208, UT 378, UT 405].
