The system dynamics cluster at the nexus of supply, recycle, waste, evolutionary, and environmental systems is characterized by its rigorous modeling of complex, multi-actor industrial and supply networks—encompassing domains such as manufacturing, inventory, waste management, electric and automotive sectors, renewables, and megaprojects—through the use of feedback-rich simulation approaches [UT 79, UT 214, UT 93, UT 341, UT 2, UT 217, UT 110, UT 14, UT 282, UT 174, UT 167, UT 495, UT 103, UT 146, UT 98, UT 212, UT 80, UT 151, UT 8, UT 371, UT 59, UT 149, UT 171, UT 213, UT 223, UT 285, UT 433, UT 264, UT 492, UT 6, UT 44, UT 247, UT 456, UT 181, UT 286, UT 157, UT 189, UT 421, UT 483, UT 336, UT 340, UT 21, UT 26, UT 325, UT 137, UT 168, UT 114, UT 414, UT 386, UT 499, UT 124, UT 263, UT 107, UT 89, UT 457, UT 490, UT 43, UT 250, UT 343, UT 276, UT 251, UT 127, UT 33, UT 164, UT 332, UT 35, UT 49, UT 126, UT 216, UT 248, UT 309, UT 430, UT 375, UT 162, UT 71, UT 78, UT 158, UT 265, UT 364, UT 441, UT 466, UT 475, UT 228, UT 298, UT 467, UT 427, UT 422, UT 380, UT 346, UT 347, UT 348, UT 363, UT 366, UT 268, UT 108, UT 176]. Recent research highlights an increasing sophistication and diversity in applications—driven by digitalization, integration with evolutionary game theory, and advanced hybrid methodologies—that allow for nuanced examination of value creation, resilience, technological change, and environmental policy impacts within evolving supply and solid waste chains [UT 213, UT 285, UT 421, UT 59, UT 149, UT 264, UT 93, UT 214, UT 167, UT 103, UT 495, UT 332]. However, the field faces persistent challenges: encapsulating dynamic and interdependent feedbacks involving regulatory, behavioral, and technological dimensions; refining the identification and calibration of critical variables and scenario conditions; and bridging the gap between simulation insights and actionable policy or operational strategies, especially given uncertainties in data and the complexity of stakeholder interactions [UT 336, UT 167, UT 189, UT 103, UT 495, UT 371, UT 217, UT 151, UT 212, UT 492, UT 264, UT 59, UT 157, UT 499, UT 386]. Research opportunities abound in forging tighter methodological integration—by combining system dynamics with approaches such as agent-based modeling, value stream mapping, and real-time digital applications—to better capture informal sector dynamics, cross-layer optimization, and the synergetic effects of combined fiscal, technological, and regulatory interventions on manufacturing and recycle outcomes [UT 107, UT 375, UT 430, UT 44, UT 181, UT 340, UT 495, UT 228, UT 346, UT 348]. To maximize practical value, future system dynamics applications should ground model assumptions and calibration in continuous, participatory collaboration with stakeholders, utilize robust empirical data from multi-scalar industrial settings, embed scenario-based planning tools, and account for organizational learning, behavioral heterogeneity, and human factors within their structure. Enhanced digital platforms and standardization efforts are also recommended to enable real-time monitoring and adaptive decision support, ultimately fostering broader implementation and translating theoretical advances into tangible improvements in environmental, economic, and social sustainability [UT 341, UT 346, UT 93, UT 282, UT 214, UT 14, UT 171, UT 79, UT 217, UT 347, UT 286, UT 336, UT 49, UT 276, UT 371, UT 430, UT 248, UT 213, UT 223, UT 421, UT 456, UT 107, UT 89, UT 343, UT 414, UT 463, UT 364, UT 422, UT 358].
