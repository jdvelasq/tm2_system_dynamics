The system dynamics thematic cluster focusing on environmental, construction, healthcare, and municipal solid waste management is characterized by the explicit simulation, analysis, and optimization of waste streams across diverse settings, with an emphasis on capturing the interconnected environmental, economic, and health consequences of waste practices [UT 953][UT 891][UT 827][UT 525][UT 561][UT 997][UT 819][UT 619][UT 644][UT 1000]. Recent research trends demonstrate a decisive movement from static, sector-specific models toward integrated, scenario-based simulations that embed feedback loops among waste generation, segregation, reduction, recycling, and disposal; these models consider stakeholder behaviors and regulatory dynamics, supporting robust decision-making amid urbanization, rising construction activity, and intensifying environmental pressures [UT 997][UT 626][UT 510][UT 806]. However, persistent challenges include the conceptual and technical complexities of accurately representing stakeholder and informal sector interactions, social and economic trade-offs, and the absence of harmonized definitions and frameworks for critical concepts like ecological security [UT 997][UT 510][UT 1000][UT 826][UT 525]. Data limitations, particularly regarding population dynamics and parameter uncertainty, further constrain model fidelity and the translation of findings into actionable interventions [UT 644][UT 626][UT 806][UT 847][UT 741]. These hurdles highlight important research opportunities: advancing multi-method modeling by integrating system dynamics with agent-based or discrete-event approaches; broadening model boundaries to include regulatory, behavioral, and informal-sector factors; and employing real-time, participatory, and geo-tagged data streams for dynamic calibration as a means to foster model relevance in rapidly changing environments [UT 953][UT 525][UT 997][UT 819][UT 530][UT 561][UT 891][UT 827]. Enhanced scenario analysis across urban, regional, and national scales, as well as context-sensitive comparative studies, will enrich understanding of institutional and cultural determinants shaping intervention efficacy [UT 510][UT 826][UT 1000]. To enhance practical value, researchers should prioritize participatory modeling, empirical validation with longitudinal and locally relevant data, and the establishment of iterative feedback mechanisms for model refinement, thus ensuring contextual sensitivity, stakeholder legitimacy, and the scalable deployment of sustainable, evidence-informed waste management solutions at the nexus of environmental, economic, and public health imperatives [UT 953][UT 626][UT 997][UT 819][UT 827][UT 510][UT 741][UT 619][UT 644][UT 1000].
