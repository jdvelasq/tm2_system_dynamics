The application of system dynamics within the context of nuclear safety and reactor operations is characterized by its capacity to capture the intricacies and evolving risks inherent in nuclear energy systems through feedback-driven, simulation-based modeling [UT 655][UT 568][UT 772][UT 593][UT 950][UT 502][UT 951]. At its core, this thematic cluster leverages system dynamics as a methodological foundation for systematically assessing nuclear power plant reliability, safety, and risk, incorporating both technical and human–organizational interdependencies [UT 970][UT 772]. Recent trends reflect a maturation of the field, with an expanded evaluative lens that integrates economic and operational considerations alongside traditional safety metrics, and with scenario simulations addressing not only typical operational failures and maintenance actions but also external threats such as seismic events and cyberattacks [UT 655][UT 568][UT 970][UT 950][UT 502][UT 593][UT 951]. The evolution of dynamic modeling platforms, such as Vensim and iThink, has enabled more robust scenario-based analyses, driving a shift toward holistic evaluations that account for social, organizational, and economic dimensions of nuclear safety [UT 772][UT 659][UT 655][UT 950][UT 502]. Notwithstanding these advances, several persistent challenges remain, including the methodological difficulty of modeling hybrid systems with both continuous and stochastic elements, the integration of multifaceted technical–organizational causal structures, and the reliable simulation of rare, catastrophic events amid limited data availability [UT 659][UT 970][UT 655][UT 950][UT 502][UT 772]. Research opportunities therefore reside in developing advanced, user-friendly tools for hybrid modeling, formalizing multi-state reliability frameworks, improving scenario-generation algorithms, and further clarifying the relationships between safety, economics, and operational policy [UT 772][UT 970][UT 659][UT 655][UT 950][UT 502][UT 568][UT 593][UT 951]. To maximize practical impact, future work should emphasize model transparency, broaden stakeholder engagement through accessible modeling interfaces and training, and continually benchmark dynamic simulations against empirical safety incident data, thereby enabling nuclear sector decision-makers to proactively manage risk with an integrated, evidence-based approach [UT 659][UT 772][UT 970][UT 568][UT 593][UT 951][UT 655][UT 950][UT 502].
