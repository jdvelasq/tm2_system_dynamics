The product-focused cluster within system dynamics is fundamentally defined by its commitment to modeling the structural and dynamic complexities inherent in product phenomena, with an emphasis on both development and market diffusion processes. Foundational research in the area begins with system dynamics enhancements of classical models such as the Bass diffusion framework, systematically integrating constructs like competitive interaction, cost learning effects, and profit analytics to advance beyond static representations toward comprehensive, dynamic simulations [UT 828][UT 552][UT 769][UT 871]. Recent trends reveal a pronounced methodological shift: while traditional, linear models remain referential, contemporary research continuously extends them to better capture multi-product competition, nonlinear feedback mechanisms, and complex interdependencies—including the integration of behavioral and psychological factors for richer scenario analyses, notably in sectors such as tourism and transport [UT 731]. This progression brings notable challenges, including persistent difficulties in parameterizing models due to empirical data limitations, validating dynamic system outputs in uncertain and evolving environments, and ensuring that model complexity does not undermine interpretability or practical application [UT 552][UT 769][UT 871][UT 828][UT 731]. These methodological constraints are particularly acute in global-scale, policy-relevant fields, where long-term projections are susceptible to data scarcity and traditional econometric approaches prove insufficient. Nevertheless, research opportunities abound: advancing more robust empirical calibration, incorporating modular architectures for generic model development, and systematically integrating behavioral economics and psychological modeling are crucial pathways forward [UT 828][UT 552][UT 769][UT 871][UT 731]. Addressing underexplored topics—such as the simulation of supply chain complexities and the dynamic interplay between technology diffusion, regulation, and consumer behavior—has the potential to substantially elevate the theory and practice of product system dynamics. To enhance practical value, scholars recommend continual empirical validation of models, close engagement with practitioners to ensure actionable relevance, and standardization of extensions for cost learning and competitive dynamics. This approach ensures that system dynamics not only retains conceptual rigor but also delivers actionable, credible solutions to the real-world challenges facing modern product systems [UT 828][UT 552][UT 769][UT 871][UT 731].
