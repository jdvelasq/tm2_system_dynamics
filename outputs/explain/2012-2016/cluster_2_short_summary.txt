This cluster is distinguished by its comprehensive application of system dynamics to analyze the complex, multi-echelon interactions that define modern supply, inventory, and manufacturing systems—spanning suppliers, manufacturers, distributors, recyclers, and end-users, with a special focus on inventory management, supply-demand alignment, and risk phenomena such as the bullwhip effect [UT 994][UT 929][UT 922][UT 525][UT 953][UT 729][UT 927][UT 629][UT 665][UT 546][UT 585][UT 967][UT 712][UT 898][UT 935][UT 516][UT 642][UT 925][UT 939][UT 786][UT 674][UT 857][UT 653][UT 505][UT 856][UT 852][UT 542][UT 754]. Recent research trends reveal a transition toward increasingly holistic and empirically anchored models that integrate closed-loop and recycling processes—such as remanufacturing and reverse logistics—while formalizing the roles of manufacturers, distributors, and recyclers in dynamic, digitally enabled networks [UT 953][UT 525][UT 927][UT 546][UT 713][UT 997][UT 786][UT 706]. However, conceptual and methodological challenges persist, including modeling the intricate interdependencies among supply chain actors, capturing nonlinearities and uncertain risk propagation (especially regarding inventory volatility and recycling delays), and overcoming obstacles related to data reliability and behavioral complexity [UT 994][UT 729][UT 927][UT 935][UT 642][UT 647][UT 629][UT 665][UT 766][UT 712][UT 939][UT 997][UT 546][UT 898][UT 505][UT 599][UT 852][UT 542][UT 653]. Research opportunities abound in integrating advanced digitalization, hybrid methodologies (e.g., agent-based models, machine learning), and behavioral formalization to enable real-time, adaptive inventory and risk management, particularly in closed and circular supply systems that require coordinated strategies across manufacturing, supply, and recycling domains [UT 822][UT 647][UT 786][UT 857][UT 505][UT 994][UT 953][UT 525][UT 927][UT 935][UT 599][UT 852][UT 822][UT 766][UT 913][UT 546][UT 615][UT 706][UT 997][UT 647][UT 585][UT 674][UT 653][UT 542]. Practically, to maximize the value and practical impact of this cluster, a stronger emphasis must be placed on data-sharing, inventory visibility, integration of digital workflows, and systematic documentation of policy impacts—especially surrounding recycling, inventory, and bullwhip risk. Formal engagement of informal actors, sustained organizational learning, and broader performance evaluations—including customer satisfaction and environmental impacts—will ensure system dynamics interventions remain both robust and socially relevant, ultimately enhancing resilience, sustainability, and value creation across the interconnected supply, manufacturing, and recycling networks.
